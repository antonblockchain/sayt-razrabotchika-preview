Designing personal accounts for transactional services

Why role-based thinking matters more than screens when you design accounts for users, partners and corporate clients.

October 1, 2026 · 4 min read

A personal account is rarely a single interface. An end user, a partner and a corporate client may look at the same operation but need very different actions, data and levels of detail.

We start with roles, not screens. For each role we describe what they need to see, what they are allowed to change and which events they must be notified about. This becomes the basis for both the interface and the permission model.

Statuses deserve special attention. Users should always understand where an operation is, what happens next and whether anything is expected from them. Clear status language reduces support requests more than any help section.

Finally, design for growth. New roles, reports and actions will appear after launch. A design system and a consistent navigation model let the account grow without being redesigned every year.
